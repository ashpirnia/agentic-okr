# ADR-0009 — Guardrail metrics, restraints and anti-targets

**Status:** Accepted — amended 2026-08-07, see Amendments
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Deciders:** Ash, Claude

## Context

These are the fields the whole project exists for. Objectives and key results are standard OKR machinery that any tool has; the guardrails, restraints and anti-targets are where the interpretive layer — the judgment humans supply silently and agents do not — actually gets written down.

Three constraints arrive from earlier decisions:

- [ADR-0005](0005-node-types.md) made `Metric` a first-class node and kept `Guardrail`, `Restraint` and `AntiTarget` embedded in the key result that sets them.
- [ADR-0001](0001-git-holds-intent.md) made a metric's identity the join key across three stores — git's intent, the Conductor's measurement config, the Shepherd's readings — and put connection details firmly outside the OKR repo.
- The reader of a pull request is a goal owner, not a developer ([ADR-0002](0002-two-repos.md)).

## Decision

```yaml
# metrics.yaml
metrics:
  - id: reopen_rate_7d
    definition: Share of resolved tickets reopened by the customer within 7 days
    unit: ratio
```

```yaml
# within a key result
guardrails:
  - metric: reopen_rate_7d
    must_not_exceed: 0.08
  - metric: csat
    must_not_fall_below: 4.2

anti_targets:
  - description: Mass-close tickets with a canned reply and let the customer chase you
    origin: authored
    restraint: A ticket may not be closed with a boilerplate "please reopen if this persists"
    watched_by: [reopen_rate_7d]
```

*The shape above reflects Amendment 1. The Decision text below describes restraints as a separate top-level list; that was superseded.*

### Metric carries `id`, `definition` and `unit` — nothing else

**The measurement window is part of identity, not a field.** `reopen_rate_7d` and `reopen_rate_30d` are two metrics. A `window` field would let two entries share an `id` while meaning different things, which is precisely what a join key cannot tolerate: the Shepherd reads two distinct series and must be able to tell them apart by identity alone.

**There is no source-system field**, not even as a free-text hint. Where a value comes from is measurement configuration, which ADR-0001 Amendment 1 assigned to the Conductor.

**`unit` is a non-empty free string**, not an enum. Organisations measure in ratios, hours, currencies, rating scales, story points and things nobody has thought of; an enum would be permanently incomplete and would push authors into picking the nearest wrong value.

It earns its place on legibility rather than on machine use — nothing in v1 reads it. `must_not_exceed: 0.08` on `reopen_rate_7d` is ambiguous until `unit: ratio` tells the reader it means 8%, and that reader is a support lead approving a pull request. A field that exists so a non-developer can interpret a threshold correctly is doing the job this project is for.

**`target` is numeric**, and applies only to `type: metric` key results.

### Guardrails carry the comparison explicitly

`must_not_exceed` and `must_not_fall_below`, on the guardrail, rather than a bare `threshold` interpreted against a direction stored on the metric.

Exactly one of the two must be present.

### Restraints are prose, and remain distinct from anti-targets

A restraint is **normative**: a rule stating what may not be done. An anti-target is **descriptive**: a prediction of the move someone would make to hit the number while betraying its spirit.

They pair naturally — "mass-close with a canned reply" is the move, "may not close with boilerplate" is the prohibition — but they are produced differently. The Champion *generates* anti-targets by asking how a goal could be gamed; the owner *declares* restraints. Both are published vocabulary in piece 3.

### Anti-targets carry `description`, `origin` and optional `watched_by`

**`origin: authored | proposed`.** An authored anti-target came from the goal owner. A proposed one was generated by the Champion and confirmed by a human. The value set is extensible; the Shepherd will add `discovered` when it exists.

**`watched_by` lists metrics that would detect the move.** Optional, and referencing metrics rather than guardrails because [ADR-0005](0005-node-types.md) left guardrails embedded and therefore unaddressable. The validator checks that each listed metric is actually guarded on this key result — a `watched_by` pointing at an unguarded metric is a false sense of coverage, which is worse than none.

**An anti-target with no `watched_by` is a completeness signal, not an error.** Some gaming moves genuinely cannot be measured, and forcing a guardrail would produce fake ones.

## Consequences

**Metric naming becomes load-bearing in a way authors will not anticipate.** Whether the window belongs in the identifier is not a style question — get it wrong and the Shepherd silently merges two series or splits one. The glossary and `okr init`'s scaffold both need to make the convention obvious, because nothing in the schema can enforce it.

**A guardrail reads correctly in a pull request with no lookup.** `must_not_exceed: 0.08` is complete on its own line. Under a `threshold` plus metric-direction design, a reviewer would have to open `metrics.yaml` to know whether 0.08 was a ceiling or a floor — and the reviewer is a support lead, on a phone, approving a change to their own team's goals.

**`watched_by` makes an unmonitored anti-target visible.** An anti-target nobody can detect is a worry rather than a control, and until now nothing would have said so. This gives the completeness rubric its sharpest single check, and it is a strong facilitation prompt: *"you have named the move — what would tell you it was happening?"*

**The `authored` / `proposed` distinction is the article's evidence.** The Champion's claim is that it draws out anti-targets the owner had not written down. Without provenance, a processed spec is just a longer spec and the reader has to take the claim on trust. With it, the before-and-after shows exactly which lines the agent surfaced.

**Provenance also creates an integrity obligation.** `proposed` must mean *proposed by the agent and confirmed by a human*, never *generated and written*. If the Champion can set it without confirmation, the field becomes a claim about facilitation that facilitation did not do. Phase 2 must make this impossible rather than merely discouraged.

**Restraints and anti-targets will be confused by authors.** The distinction is real but fine, and some people will put a prohibition in `anti_targets` or a predicted move in `restraints`. Accepted, because the vocabulary is published across three articles and the two are produced by different acts. The Champion's prompts are the mitigation: it asks for them separately and at different moments.

**Nothing validates that a restraint is meaningful.** "Do not do bad things" passes. Prose fields cannot be checked for substance by a schema, which is precisely why the Champion exists — this is the boundary between what a validator can do and what facilitation must.

## Alternatives rejected

**A `window` field on the metric.** Reads naturally — `id: reopen_rate`, `window: 7d` — and avoids encoding data into identifiers, which is normally poor practice. Rejected because identity is the join key across three stores. Two metrics with the same `id` and different windows would be indistinguishable to the Conductor's measurement source and to the Shepherd's time series, producing either a silent merge of two series or an ambiguous lookup. This is the one place where encoding into the identifier is correct, because the identifier's job is to be the thing three systems agree on.

**A source-system hint on the metric.** The plan originally sketched this as free text, and it is genuinely useful documentation — a reader wants to know where a number comes from. Rejected because it is measurement configuration under a documentation label, and because a `source: zendesk` field is an open invitation to `endpoint:` and then to credentials. ADR-0001 drew that boundary deliberately; a field that sits just inside it will not stay there. Authors who want to say it can say it in `definition`.

**Direction on the metric, bare threshold on the guardrail.** More normalised: a metric knows whether lower is better, so the comparison is derivable and cannot be stated inconsistently across two guardrails on the same metric. Rejected on legibility — the reviewer would need a second file to interpret a threshold — and because metrics with no natural direction would need a special case anyway. Normalisation is worth less here than a line that reads correctly on its own.

**Merging restraints and anti-targets into one list.** Simpler, and it removes a distinction authors will get wrong. Rejected because they are outputs of different acts: one is elicited by asking "how would someone game this?", the other declared by an owner stating a rule. Collapsing them would remove the Champion's sharpest prompt, and it would contradict vocabulary already published in three articles.

**Restraint as a field on the anti-target.** Models the pairing directly and makes the anti-target the primary object. Genuinely tidy. Rejected because not every restraint originates from a predicted gaming move — some are policy, or the residue of an incident — and nesting would force authors to invent an anti-target to hang a rule on.

**Deferring provenance entirely.** ADR-0005's own rule says a field is cheap to add later, and nothing in v1's *schema* work reads it. Rejected because Phase 2 is inside v1, and the Champion proposing anti-targets that a human confirms is the demonstration the article rests on. The field would be needed within weeks, and adding it after the reference examples and demo dataset were authored would mean rewriting both.

**Including `discovered` in the value set now.** Completes the set and lets the demo stage a discovered anti-target to illustrate the loop. Rejected because nothing discovers anything in v1: a staged value would be illustration dressed as evidence, in an article whose credibility rests on being straight about what is built. The Shepherd adds it when the Shepherd exists, which costs one enum value.

**Guardrails as first-class nodes so `watched_by` could reference them directly.** More precise than pointing at a metric, since it would name the specific threshold. Rejected — ADR-0005 settled this — and the metric reference is sufficient because the validator checks the metric is guarded *on this key result*, which is the property that matters.

## Amendments

### Amendment 1 · 2026-08-07 — Restraint nests inside the anti-target

The original decision kept `restraints` and `anti_targets` as parallel lists, justified as "normative versus descriptive." That distinction survives an ADR but not contact with an author:

- anti-target: *Mass-close tickets with a canned reply and let the customer chase you*
- restraint: *A ticket may not be closed with a boilerplate "please reopen if this persists"*

Same behaviour, different grammatical mood. Asked to fill in both fields, a goal owner writes one sentence twice. A schema that invites that has a real defect, and no amount of documentation fixes a distinction a reader can only apply once they already understand it.

**The real difference was mis-sited.** It is not about the sentence's mood, it is about *what defends against the move*:

- A **rule** is checked against an agent's configuration, statically, before anything runs — piece 3's lint compares an agent's task definition against exactly this.
- A **metric** is watched while the fleet runs — the Shepherd's job.

So a restraint and a `watched_by` are not siblings of the anti-target. They are its two defences, one static and one dynamic.

**The decision.** `restraint` becomes an optional field on the anti-target, alongside `watched_by`. The top-level `restraints` list is removed.

```yaml
anti_targets:
  - description: Mass-close tickets with a canned reply and let the customer chase you
    origin: authored
    restraint: A ticket may not be closed with a boilerplate "please reopen if this persists"
    watched_by: [reopen_rate_7d]
```

**Consequences.**

*"Which field does this go in?" stops being a question.* There is one place to name a gaming move, and two places to say what stops it.

*The completeness check gets sharper than the original.* The first version flagged an anti-target with no `watched_by`. The better check is an anti-target with **neither** a restraint nor a watching metric — named but wholly undefended. One defence missing is a gap; both missing is a worry written down and nothing more.

*The two defences map onto the two enforcement points*, which is a property worth having in the schema's shape: the restraint is what the Conductor lints against at wiring time, the metric is what the Shepherd watches at run time. A move defended only statically survives an agent that finds a different route to it; a move defended only dynamically is caught after it has already happened.

*The term survives, refined.* "Restraint clause" remains published vocabulary from piece 3, now defined as the rule forbidding a named anti-target rather than as a free-standing instrument. The glossary states the refinement rather than implying the articles said it.

**Reversing an earlier rejection.** The original rejected nesting because "not every restraint originates from a predicted gaming move — some are policy, or the residue of an incident." That does not hold. A policy unconnected to gaming *this* key result does not belong in the key result's spec at all, and an incident-derived rule has an implied move behind it — the thing that caused the incident. Neither produces a restraint with no anti-target.
